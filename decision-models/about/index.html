About the Reading bench

Our reading comprehension and grammar test: 2,198 questions answered by 40 decision models, with chat models for comparison. A decision model scores each answer option and picks one. Grammar questions test whether a model knows a rule and can apply it. Every answer is public.

The questions come from a published book; their text stays unpublished, as the publisher owns it. Every setup's answer to every question is in answers.json. The Method page has the full study: the questions, the answer key, the runs and the tests.

How the scores work

  • What is scored. 2,198 questions written in Turkish, not translated: 656 reading and 1,542 grammar. Each answer is right or wrong against the answer key; an invalid answer counts as wrong.
  • How models answer. A decision model scores each answer option and picks one, with a confidence for it. A chat model gets the question and its options in a prompt and replies with the letter of its answer.
  • What a setup is. One model with one setting: its reasoning level, how many questions go in one request (1 or 16), where it ran (a provider's API, a subscription or our GPUs) and whether it searched the web. 80 setups: 40 decision-model and 40 chat-model setups.
  • How differences were tested. Paired tests on the same questions, with Holm's correction for the number of comparisons. Setups that share a letter in the ranking were not told apart by the paired test.
  • What cost means. An estimate in dollars per 1,000 questions, not a bill: list prices and recorded token use for API setups; for our GPUs, a list price for the same model or weights, or the GPU time.

The rest is on the Method page.

Notes behind the scores

Ranks show observed scores; small gaps need not be meaningful.

Costs: API list prices and recorded token use. Setups on our own GPUs: the maker's list price for the same model (each value's tooltip names it); our hardware cost is not measured. On our GPUs, estimated at a public host's list price for the same weights (the host, the date read and any precision difference are on each setup's page): an API-equivalent estimate, not measured spending. On our GPUs with no public price for the same weights, estimated from GPU time: the run's request time x the GPUs its server held x Runpod's on-demand price for an RTX PRO 6000, $2.49 per GPU-hour (read 5 November 2026): a cloud-equivalent estimate, not measured spending. ≤ Upper-bound estimate: one $0.001 web search counted per request; Perplexity bills only completed searches. * Measured on a sample (200 questions).

The leaderboard rules

Row rule: each model's highest reasoning setting (none or off < minimal < low < medium < high < xhigh < max; on above off for a model with only on and off); a decision model has no setting. Tie-break: several setups at that setting give the row to the earliest run (the lowest run id), never to the higher score. A model with one setup uses it. Every value of a row and of its details box comes from that one chosen setup; its other setups are under the row's expander and under All setups. A setup with web search is its own row; different version names are separate rows; a dated checkpoint joins another model's row only through the map below, never by a similar name.

The dated-checkpoint map (page_config.json, leaderboard.dated_checkpoints), the only model IDs listed under another: deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017 is listed under deepseek-v4.1-flash, by this map. The row shows the original; the checkpoint's setups sit under its other settings.

Ranks show observed scores; small gaps need not be meaningful. # is the rank among the models in view: All ranks every model; Chat models and Decision models rank within their kind. Tied scores share a rank.

Each bar runs from 0 to 100%; its whisker is the Wilson 95% interval of the accuracy, the interval each setup's page gives. The paired tests behind the full ranking's letters are on Method.

Not measured or "Not run" in a column: the setup has no such value; the details box leaves such a line out.

Summary

Among decision models, none led on every measure:

  • Accuracy: REAX 1.3 and Liquid d1-ultra, tied and clearly ahead at 77.9% and 77.1%; then Decider v1.2 (API, with web), GLiDE v1.2, GLiDE, vLLM-SR d3 and d3-ultra (tied), Drex Max v1 and 1.1, Mammoth-1, and Deciders v1.1 and v1.2 (local GPU), at 66.9% to 68.7%.
  • Calibration gap, as shipped: Liquid d1.1 had the smallest gap among decision models.
  • Better than a flat guess, after refit: Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2 (API) at 32.1%, then Open-Jev 27B v1.1 (31.9%), then SubQ Snap 1.1 and Perplexity Decider 27B v1.1 (31.5% each). We did not test whether the gaps between them are real.
  • Estimated saving: d1-ultra, the biggest in 10 of the 16 LLM setups; REAX 1.3 in the other 6.
  • Reading vs grammar: decision models got up to 92.4% of the reading questions right, but at most 71.8% of the grammar questions.
