When should you trust a decision model?

Our reading comprehension and grammar test: 2,198 questions answered by 39 decision models, with chat models for comparison. A decision model scores each answer option and picks one. Grammar questions test whether a model knows a rule and can apply it. Every answer is public.

The questions are in native Turkish (not translated).

Beside the ranking: Web grounding, Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2 (API) against Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2 (with web). PWS-Decisions, the decision models on Parallel's benchmark of dated web facts.

Results 2522aafa, updated 5 November 2026; answer key version 2.

Among decision models, none led on every measure:

  • Accuracy: Liquid d1-ultra, clearly ahead at 77.1%; then Decider v1.2 (with web), GLiDE v1.2, GLiDE, vLLM-SR d3 and d3-ultra (tied), Drex Max v1 and 1.1, Mammoth-1, and Deciders v1.1 and v1.2, at 66.9% to 68.7%.
  • Calibration gap, as shipped: Liquid d1.1 had the smallest gap among decision models.
  • Better than a flat guess, after refit: Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2 (API) at 32.1%, then Open-Jev 27B v1.1 (31.9%), then SubQ Snap 1.1 and Perplexity Decider 27B v1.1 (31.5% each). We did not test whether the gaps between them are real.
  • Estimated saving: d1-ultra, the biggest in all 16 LLM setups.
  • Reading vs grammar: decision models got up to 92.1% of the reading questions right, but at most 70.7% of the grammar questions.
