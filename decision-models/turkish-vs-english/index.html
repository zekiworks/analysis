Risk and coverage

How often each model answers and how often its answers are wrong, on the same 547 kept questions in both languages: with the option, as the model chose it, and without it, by holding back the answers below a confidence score.

Method

Answered = right + wrong (an invalid answer or one without a score is never answered). Coverage = answered / 547. Error rate among answered (risk) = wrong / answered. Abstained: for a run with the option, the times it chose the option; for a confidence point, 547 minus answered minus invalid. Each confidence curve has one point per distinct score of the run on these questions (answer when the score, the recorded probability of the chosen option, is at least that score): answers with equal scores enter together, so coverage moves in jumps and every point is one the run achieved; a model that reports its probabilities with few decimals has many equal scores, so its curve has fewer points, moves in larger steps and can start well above 0% coverage (each curve is drawn from its first point). Every series counts these questions only (a run on the full bank is restricted to them) and is each model's first run. Turkish curves: the Text tab run; English curves: the first English run. Turkish with “Bilmiyorum” is the Turkish side of the English “I don't know” comparison or, for a model outside it, its Hallucination tab run, restricted to these; Turkish with “I don't know” is the option's word control on the Hallucination tab's questions, restricted to these; a model without such a run is Not measured.

All series are counted on the same kept reading questions; a full-bank run is restricted to them; first runs only. Answered = right + wrong (an invalid answer or one without a score is never answered). Coverage = answered / kept questions. Risk = error rate among answered = wrong / answered. Abstained: for a native point, the times the model chose the option; for a curve point, kept questions - answered - invalid. A native point is the run with its abstention option (Turkish "Bilmiyorum", Turkish "I don't know" as the wording control, English "I don't know"); Turkish "Bilmiyorum" is the Turkish side of the English "I don't know" comparison or, for a model outside it, its Hallucination tab run; a missing one is Not measured. A curve is one five-option run held back by its recorded score (the probability of the chosen option): one point per distinct score s, answering when the score is at least s; answers with equal scores enter together, so coverage moves in jumps and every point is an achieved one; all distinct points kept, ordered by coverage.

About this test

The 13 decision models of Artificial Analysis's Decision Index (its post of 22 October 2026) that we benchmark, and the ten most accurate decision models on the Text tab on 2 November 2026, seven of them not in that index: 20 in all. 17 of them are compared here; the other three are listed under the results with their reasons.

The Text tab's reading questions (units 1 to 6; the grammar questions are left out) were translated into English by Claude Opus 5.5. The same model flagged the questions whose answer depends on Turkish wording; the 109 flagged questions are left out and the other 547 kept. Each of the 17 compared models answered the kept questions in English and is compared with its own Text tab run on the same 547 questions.

Download: results.json (the comparison's numbers, under english_reading), answers.json (each English run's answers, under english_runs), english-reading.json (the translator, its prompts, the flags, the question units and each question's hash of its English text; no question text) and kept-question-ids.txt (the 547 kept questions' IDs, one per line).

Our method

The translation

The translator is Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5) at effort high; route: Claude Code CLI, Claude subscription, Claude Code version 2.1.312. Its maker has no decision model on our board. It was told to translate faithfully, keep the option letters and their order, not solve the question and not add or remove information (translation prompt version 2976d0f96197).

The flags

In a separate request, the same model was shown each Turkish question, its English translation and the keyed option, and asked whether answering it correctly depends on Turkish wording, with the reasons (flag prompt version 08a5c70222aa). It flagged 109 of the 656 questions; they are left out, and 547 are kept. By reason, as a flagged question can have several:

  • word or phrase meaning: 76
  • idiom: 44
  • sound: 5
  • marked words: 55
  • other: 13

No person checked the translations or the flags, and this is a limit of the method: a translation error, or a question whose answer still depends on Turkish wording that the model did not flag, would stay among the 547 kept questions and could move a model's accuracy in English. Both prompts in full are in english-reading.json and runner/translate_reading.py.

The structural check

A script (runner/translate_reading.py, its check step) then compared each translation with its Turkish original: a problem is a missing translation, option letters other than the original's, an empty text or a different count of underline markup; a warning is a change in the numbering or the numbers, Turkish letters in the English, or an English text under half or over 2.5 times the Turkish length. It found problems in 1 question (3043), already left out by the flags, so it left out no further question; its 159 warnings leave out nothing. In all, the checks left out 109 of the 656 questions and kept 547: their IDs are in kept-question-ids.txt, one per line, and in english-reading.json.

Why the grammar questions are not translated

The Text tab's 1,542 grammar questions test Turkish grammar itself, so a translation would test something else, not the same question in another language. Only the 656 reading questions were translated.

The site never publishes question text, Turkish or English: the publisher owns the questions. english-reading.json holds instead each reading question's ID, unit, whether it was kept, its flag reasons and the SHA-256 of its English text, with the translator and both prompts, so that a copy of these translations can be checked against it. The rates and how to read them

Each answer is right, wrong, the option or invalid. Out of the 547 kept questions, accuracy is right / 547; Answered is (right + wrong) / 547, the share of questions the model answered (the attempt rate), higher is better; Wrong of answers is wrong / (right + wrong), the share of its answers that were wrong, lower is better, and Not measured when it answered none. These two lead the table, with their counts under them. The non-hallucination rate, after them, is the option / (the option + wrong): among the answers that were not right, the share where the model said it did not know. As the Hallucination tab says, it is not a direct measure of what the model does not know: a model that always chose the option would reach 100% with no right answers, so read it with the attempt rate. With neither a wrong answer nor the option, the rate is undefined (Not measured). The mean probability is the probability the model gave the option, averaged over its answers that report option probabilities. The paired test is the exact McNemar test on the questions only one of the two first runs got wrong, with p adjusted by Holm's method over the 13 models. No new combined score is made from these rates.

Our method with “I don't know” in both languages

What is the same

In both languages the sixth option, F, is written “I don't know”, always the last option, never shuffled and never correct, and the instructions get the same added sentence: “If you do not know the answer, choose I don't know.” Both sides are counted on the same 547 kept questions.

What differs

The language of the question and its options (the English translation, as in the accuracy comparison above, against the Turkish original); the one word of the instructions that names the language of the question; and the options heading, “Seçenekler:” against “Options:”. The instructions of the Turkish runs:

Which option is the correct answer to this Turkish multiple-choice question? If you do not know the answer, choose I don't know.

The instructions of the English runs:

Which option is the correct answer to this English multiple-choice question? If you do not know the answer, choose I don't know.

The runs

The Turkish side is the Hallucination tab's option-word run (the option's word) on all 2,198 Turkish questions, counted on the 547 kept questions only: Liquid d1-ultra (run 511), Fastino GLiDE v1.2 (run 525), Mammoth-1 (run 519), vLLM-SR d3 (run 544), Drex Max v1 (run 542), vLLM-SR d3-ultra (run 540), Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2 (run 531), Drex Max 1.1 (run 513), SubQ Snap 1.1 (run 512) and Elephant-1 (run 515). The test

A wrong answer is a valid choice other than “I don't know” that is not right. The paired test is the exact McNemar test on the questions only one of the two runs got wrong, with p adjusted by Holm's method over the seven models with both runs: a family of its own, separate from every other family on the site. The comparison with “Bilmiyorum” in Turkish stays as published in the section above (its table).
