About this test

The 13 decision models of Artificial Analysis's Decision Index (its post of 22 October 2026) that we benchmark, and the ten most accurate decision models on the Text tab on 2 November 2026, seven of them not in that index: 20 in all. 14 of them are compared here; the other six are listed under the results with their reasons.

The Text tab's reading questions (units 1 to 6; the grammar questions are left out) were translated into English by Claude Opus 5.5. The same model flagged the questions whose answer depends on Turkish wording; the 109 flagged questions are left out and the other 547 kept. Each of the 14 compared models answered the kept questions in English and is compared with its own Text tab run on the same 547 questions.

Download: results.json (the comparison's numbers, under english_reading), answers.json (each English run's answers, under english_runs), english-reading.json (the translator, its prompts, the flags, the question units and each question's hash of its English text; no question text) and kept-question-ids.txt (the 547 kept questions' IDs, one per line).

Our method

The translation

The translator is Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5) at effort high; route: Claude Code CLI, Claude subscription, Claude Code version 2.1.312. Its maker has no decision model on our board. It was told to translate faithfully, keep the option letters and their order, not solve the question and not add or remove information (translation prompt version 2976d0f96197).

The flags

In a separate request, the same model was shown each Turkish question, its English translation and the keyed option, and asked whether answering it correctly depends on Turkish wording, with the reasons (flag prompt version 08a5c70222aa). It flagged 109 of the 656 questions; they are left out, and 547 are kept. By reason, as a flagged question can have several:

  • word or phrase meaning: 76
  • idiom: 44
  • sound: 5
  • marked words: 55
  • other: 13

No person checked the translations or the flags, and this is a limit of the method: a translation error, or a question whose answer still depends on Turkish wording that the model did not flag, would stay among the 547 kept questions and could move a model's accuracy in English. Both prompts in full are in english-reading.json and runner/translate_reading.py.

The structural check

A script (runner/translate_reading.py, its check step) then compared each translation with its Turkish original: a problem is a missing translation, option letters other than the original's, an empty text or a different count of underline markup; a warning is a change in the numbering or the numbers, Turkish letters in the English, or an English text under half or over 2.5 times the Turkish length. It found problems in 1 question (3043), already left out by the flags, so it left out no further question; its 159 warnings leave out nothing. In all, the checks left out 109 of the 656 questions and kept 547: their IDs are in kept-question-ids.txt, one per line, and in english-reading.json.

Why the grammar questions are not translated

The Text tab's 1,542 grammar questions test Turkish grammar itself, so a translation would test something else, not the same question in another language. Only the 656 reading questions were translated.

Asking the decision models

Each model is asked exactly as on the Text tab, with its own settings, one question per request, and with its option probabilities, except for one word of the System One instructions, the options heading and the question itself. The Text tab's instructions:

Which option is the correct answer to this Turkish multiple-choice question?

The English runs' instructions:

Which option is the correct answer to this English multiple-choice question?

The options follow the heading “Options:” instead of “Seçenekler:”, and the question and its options are the English translation. Prompt version 47f5776e71db; every English run used the translations with SHA-256 bf0d47cd385a93050c65906977e199ef7e45d5848dbbdef2bc8d124f4d5abf68. Liquid d1 was asked as d1, while its Turkish runs used d1:free, because Liquid's API no longer serves that name; its model list still names d1, released 29 September 2026. Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2, Perplexity Decider Flash v1.2 and Fastino GLiDE v1.2 made their Text tab runs on an NVIDIA DGX Spark and their English runs on our RTX PRO 6000 GPU, with the same checkpoint, server code and settings. Celeris-1 Decision was added on 3 November 2026 with one English run, although its repeated Turkish runs changed answers, for which the rule under Runs gives five.

Runs

One English run per model; five where the model's repeated Turkish runs on the Text tab changed answers. Here: five runs for Liquid d1-ultra, Liquid d1, Jev 1.14.0, Inception Mercury Decide and Microsoft-Decision-1; one for the other nine. The English runs are kept out of the leaderboard and its repeats.

The comparison

On the 547 kept questions only: accuracy is the right answers over 547 for the first English run and for the Text tab run (an invalid answer counts as not right), and the difference is English minus Turkish. The paired test is the exact McNemar test on the questions only one of the two runs got right, with p adjusted by Holm's method over the 14 models compared. Where a side has more than one run, its range is the lowest and highest accuracy over its runs, and its answers changed are the kept questions whose answer differs between them.

Why the translations are not published

The site never publishes question text, Turkish or English: the publisher owns the questions. english-reading.json holds instead each reading question's ID, unit, whether it was kept, its flag reasons and the SHA-256 of its English text, with the translator and both prompts, so that a copy of these translations can be checked against it. Translating again with runner/translate_reading.py need not give the same text word for word.

Our method with an “I don't know” option

The option

As on the Hallucination tab, each question gets a sixth option after its own five: F, “I don't know” in English, “Bilmiyorum” in Turkish. It is always the last option, never shuffled and never correct. The English reading instructions get one added sentence: “If you do not know the answer, choose I don't know.” The full instructions:

Which option is the correct answer to this English multiple-choice question? If you do not know the answer, choose I don't know.

The rates and how to read them

Each answer is right, wrong, the option or invalid. Out of the 547 kept questions, accuracy is right / 547, the attempt rate is (right + wrong) / 547, and the non-hallucination rate is the option / (the option + wrong): among the answers that were not right, the share where the model said it did not know. As the Hallucination tab says, it is not a direct measure of what the model does not know: a model that always chose the option would reach 100% with no right answers, so read it with the attempt rate. With neither a wrong answer nor the option, the rate is undefined (Not measured). The mean probability is the probability the model gave the option, averaged over its answers that report option probabilities. The paired test is the exact McNemar test on the questions only one of the two first runs got wrong, with p adjusted by Holm's method over the 13 models. No new combined score is made from these rates.
