About this test

Every question of the Text tab gets one more option after its five answers: F, “Bilmiyorum”, Turkish for “I don't know”. It is always the last option, it is never shuffled and it is never correct. The System One instructions get one added sentence: “If you do not know the answer, choose Bilmiyorum.” Everything else is as on the Text tab: the same 2,198 scored questions and answer key, one question per request, and each model's own settings from its text run.

The models: the ten most accurate decision models on the Text tab at results 25290875, plus Base Labs' Elephant-1 and Mammoth-1 and SubQ Snap 1.1 once their text runs are published. Each has one full run; a model whose repeated text runs changed answers gets five.

Download: results.json (every number on this page, under dont_know) and answers.json (each run's answers, under dont_know_runs).

What we found

Of the 11 models, 7 used “Bilmiyorum”, on 14.6% to 31.4% of the questions. The other 4, vLLM-SR d3-ultra, Drex Max v1, Perplexity Decider 27B v1.2 and vLLM-SR d3, chose it on 0 to 10 questions, although each gave it some probability: a mean of 1.5% to 2.9%, against 16.0% to 30.7% for the 7.

Liquid d1-ultra had the highest non-hallucination rate, 75.1%: of its 822 answers that were not right, 617 were “Bilmiyorum”. Mammoth-1 (68.7%) and Fastino GLiDE (59.4%) follow. With the option, the 7 were less accurate than in their five-option runs on the Text tab, by 5.3 points (Elephant-1) to 14.5 points (Liquid d1-ultra: 77.1% against 62.6%). The 4 that almost never chose it moved by -0.3 to +0.2 points.

For 6 of the 7, the model's own confidence on its five-option run, holding back on as many questions, left fewer wrong answers than “Bilmiyorum” did: 9 to 38 fewer, a non-hallucination rate 0.5 to 3.6 points higher (the derived rate in the table). Fastino GLiDE was the exception, with 21 more. For the 4 that almost never chose it, both rates are 1.4% or less. In the exact McNemar test on each model's first run, with Holm's correction over the 7, confidence left significantly fewer wrong answers for Liquid d1-ultra and Mammoth-1 (adjusted p = 0.016 each); for the other 5, the difference could be chance.

Our method

We measure hallucination by offering a way out. Every question gets “Bilmiyorum” (“I don't know”) as a sixth option, and the instructions tell the model to choose it when it does not know the answer. A hallucination is then a wrong answer: the model chose one of the question's own options, not the key, when it could have said it did not know. Everything else is the Text tab's: the questions, the answer key and each model's settings.

How it differs from our confidence method

On the Text tab a model must pick one of the five options, and we judge each answer by its confidence: the probability the model gives the option it picked (its option score). Our confidence method keeps an answer when that probability reaches a cut-off, and a stronger model answers the rest (How we chose which answers to keep). Here no cut-off decides: the model can say itself that it does not know, by choosing “Bilmiyorum”, and the rates count those choices. The two meet in the derived non-hallucination rate, which applies the confidence method to the same model's five-option run at about the same attempt rate; side by side, the two non-hallucination columns compare a model that says it does not know with the same model held back by its own confidence.

The rates

Each answer is right, wrong, “Bilmiyorum” or invalid; an invalid answer counts as none of the others. Out of all 2,198 questions:

  • Accuracy = right / 2,198.
  • Attempt rate = (right + wrong) / 2,198: how often the model chose one of the question's own options.
  • Non-hallucination rate = “Bilmiyorum” / (“Bilmiyorum” + wrong): among the answers that were wrong or “Bilmiyorum”, the share where the model said it did not know. The hallucination rate is its complement, wrong / (“Bilmiyorum” + wrong).
  • Mean probability of “Bilmiyorum”: the probability the model gave that option, averaged over its answers that report option probabilities.

How to read them

The non-hallucination rate says how a model is wrong, not how often it is right, and it is not a direct measure of what the model does not know. Read it with accuracy and the attempt rate: a model that always chose “Bilmiyorum” would reach 100% with no right answers, and one that never chose it would reach 0% as soon as it answered one question wrongly. With neither a wrong answer nor “Bilmiyorum”, there is nothing to divide by and the rate is undefined (the table shows Not measured).

The option and the instructions

The added option's exact text is “Bilmiyorum”. It is lettered after the question's own options, and the answer key is unchanged. The full System One instructions:

Which option is the correct answer to this Turkish multiple-choice question? If you do not know the answer, choose Bilmiyorum.

Prompt version 1cdd542b597f (a hash of the prompts with these instructions and this option, computed as for the Text tab's prompt version).

Derived non-hallucination rate

For comparison, each model's run with five options (its run on the Text tab, on the same questions) is made to abstain by its own confidence: it answers only where its score reaches a cut-off and abstains on the rest. The cut-off is the one whose attempt rate comes nearest the attempt rate of the run with “Bilmiyorum”; on a tie, the higher cut-off. An answer without a score never counts as an attempt. The derived non-hallucination rate is then the abstentions over the abstentions plus the wrong answers. The table shows the cut-off and the attempt rate it reached.

Repeats

One full run per model. A model whose repeated text runs changed answers gets five runs with the option. Those runs form a repeat set of their own and are never mixed with the Text tab's runs. The table shows the first run; the range covers all of them, and the changed answers are the scored questions whose answer differs between the runs.
