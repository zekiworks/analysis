Can a model tell when a risk is worth taking?

We score every backgammon decision with an engine and compare decision quality, what the input adds, confidence and cost across 41 model setups.

Language models and decision models play backgammon leagues, and GNU Backgammon scores every legal play of every decision, so a move is judged by the equity it gives away, not by who won the game. The hardest choices trade safety against gain: leaving a blot can be the best play or a needless risk. Every decision is public.

About this table

Thread 1: Decision quality under risk. How much equity each move gives away, judged by the engine, not by wins: error rate with its 95% interval, the share of engine-best plays and of blunders.

The shared positions: 2,000 standard positions (1,000 from the earlier v1 league games and 1,000 from GNUbg self-play at expert level, 200 per game phase), plus 500 Nackgammon positions and a 200-position opening control. Every standard position has 2 to 25 legal plays, so no play list is split into groups. Every setup answers the same positions; this set, not the league tables, compares setups across leagues. How they were drawn.

Error rate (PR): 500 × the mean equity a setup gives away per decision, against GNUbg 2-ply; lower is better. The band is approximate: human ratings include doubling-cube decisions and these games have none. Invalid answers are left out of the error rate and counted next to it. Failed requests: a request that fails after retries leaves no decision, so they are not counted here. League games are not the same positions for every setup, so league rows are not paired comparisons.

About this section

Hitting rolls are the numbers the model was shown: for each legal play, how many of the opponent's 36 rolls hit. Minimum risk: the fewest hitting rolls any legal play leaves. Best safe play: among the plays that leave the minimum, the one the engine rates best. Safety pays: the best safe play loses less than 0.001 equity against the engine's best play. Risk pays: it loses 0.001 or more. No hit-risk trade-off: every legal play leaves the same number of hitting rolls; these decisions are counted and left out of every share. The model took a risk if its play leaves more hitting rolls than the minimum. A share's denominator is its row's class. Full definitions: Method.
