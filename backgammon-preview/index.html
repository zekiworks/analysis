The shared positions: 2,000 standard positions (1,000 from the earlier v1 league games and 1,000 from GNUbg self-play at expert level, 200 per game phase), plus 500 Nackgammon positions and a 200-position opening control. Every standard position has 2 to 25 legal plays, so no play list is split into groups. Every setup answers the same positions; this set, not the league tables, compares setups across leagues. How they were drawn.

Error rate (PR): 500 × the mean equity a setup gives away per decision, against GNUbg 2-ply; lower is better. The band is approximate: human ratings include doubling-cube decisions and these games have none. Invalid answers are counted, never scored as the model's choice. Failed requests: a request that fails after retries leaves no decision, so they are not counted here.

Is the risk worth it?

Analysis pending shared positions

The main research question: can a model tell when a risk is worth taking? For each decision we find the safest play, the legal play that leaves the opponent the fewest hitting rolls, as shown to the model. In Risk pays decisions, the engine's best play leaves more hitting rolls than the safest play. In Safety pays decisions, a safest play is the engine's best play or loses less than 0.001 equity. Decisions where every play leaves the same number of hitting rolls offer no such choice and are left out. This measures one kind of risk, being hit; gammon risk and priming are not in it.
