Backgammon in one page

The goal. Two players race their 15 checkers around the board and off it. The first player to bear off all 15 checkers wins.

The board. The board has 24 narrow triangles, called points. Each player moves in the opposite direction. Each player's last six points are their home board.

Moving. A player rolls two dice and moves checkers by those two numbers. One checker can take both numbers, or two checkers can take one each. A roll of doubles (for example 4-4) is played four times. A checker cannot land on a point that holds two or more of the opponent's checkers.

Hitting. A single checker alone on a point is a blot. If the opponent lands on it, the blot is hit: it goes to the middle of the board (the bar). It must come back in through the opponent's home board before any other checker can move. Being hit costs a lot of time in the race.

Bearing off. When all 15 of a player's checkers are in their home board, that player can start taking them off the board.

Gammons. If the loser has borne off no checkers, the winner wins a gammon, worth 2 points. If the loser also still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board, it is a backgammon, worth 3 points.

Why this is a test of risk. Almost every turn asks the same question. A safe play leaves no blot. A bolder play leaves a blot but builds a better position. The dice decide whether the risk pays off this time. A strong player takes the risk when it is worth it on average, not only when it feels safe.

Nackgammon. Nackgammon is backgammon with a different starting position. Each player moves one checker from the 13-point and one from the 6-point to their opponent's side, so each side starts with four back checkers instead of two. We use it to test whether a model still plays well when the opening positions are new.

How the benchmark works

  • Leagues of full games. Each model setup plays full games against every other setup in its league. There are three leagues, and they are never merged.
  • One decision, one request. Every move is sent as its own request. A model sees the match score, but not the moves of earlier turns or earlier games.
  • What the model sees. Each request shows the score, the board with pip counts, the race, the dice and every legal play. Next to each play the program lists: checkers hit, points made, blots left and how many of the opponent's 36 rolls hit them, points held, longest prime and pip counts.
  • Two kinds of model. Decision models return a probability for every legal play. Chat models choose one play.
  • A program judges, not a model. GNU Backgammon 1.08 scores every legal play in every position at 2-ply. No language model judges any move.
  • Settings. Each setup is one model at one reasoning setting (off, low, medium, high). We record the time and the tokens of every decision, and its cost where a price is known.
  • Reference programs. Three programs are shown as yardsticks and are never ranked: GNUbg weak (a weak setting of the engine, played in every league), Safest and Random. In League 1, Safest picks the play that leaves the fewest hitting rolls, then the fewest blots, then the biggest pip lead. In Leagues 2 and 3, Safest and Random are computed, not played: Safest is the engine's best of the safest plays, Random the average of all legal plays.
  • Late entries. A setup added after a league finished plays that league's original field. It is ranked on the risk leaderboard, but it does not change league match-points standings, Cups, or other setups' results.
  • Everything is public. Every position, every legal play, the engine's value for each play and every model's choice.

How the scores work

  • Risk call. A position is a risk call when its legal plays leave different numbers of hitting rolls. The engine compares its best play with the best safe play: if the best play is at least 0.001 better, risk pays; otherwise safety pays.
  • Risks found. The share of risk-pays positions where the model chose a worthwhile risky play: one at least 0.001 better than the best safe play. A risky play that is not that good counts as a wrong risk, not a risk found.
  • Safe right. The share of safety-pays positions where the model played safe.
  • Risk judgement. The average of risks found and safe right, from 0 to 100. A model that always plays safe scores 50. A model that gets every call right scores 100.
  • Error rate. How much a model's moves give away, as judged by the engine: the PR scale that human players use. Lower is better. A move that matches the engine's best play costs nothing.
  • Uncertainty. Each score has a 95% interval. Intervals resample whole games, not single moves, because moves inside one game are not independent. Ranks show observed scores; small gaps need not be meaningful.

As the Method page states it

  • Who scores. GNU Backgammon 1.08 evaluates every legal play at 2-ply, in cubeless money equity (expected points per game, match score ignored). The loss of a decision is the equity of the best play minus the equity of the play made. Method: Error rate
  • Error rate. 500 × mean 2-ply equity loss per decision. Lower is better. This is the checker-play performance rating (PR) that backgammon players use. Method: Error rate
  • Risk judgement. The average of risks found and right safety, × 100. It does not depend on how many positions of each class a setup met. With both classes present, the Safest program scores exactly 50, the "always safe" line. Method: Was it the right risk?
  • Intervals. Intervals are 0.95 bootstrap intervals with 2000 resamples and a fixed seed (20261021). League decisions resample whole games, because moves within a game are not independent. Method: Statistics and verdicts
  • Different positions. Setups played different positions; league comparisons are not paired, and risk judgement is not adjusted for how hard the positions were. Method: Limits

Leaderboard rules

  • One row per model. The row uses a setting fixed by rule, not the best score: the model's highest reasoning setting. A model with one setup uses that setup.
  • Versions. Different version names (Jev 1.13.0 and 1.14.0) are separate rows. A dated checkpoint of the same model (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017) is the same model, and its row shows the original.
  • Late entries count on the leaderboard, with a "late entry" tag, because they play the same league field.
  • Order. Rows are sorted by risk judgement. The rank is a sort order, not a verdict. Ranks show observed scores; small gaps need not be meaningful.
  • Tap a row to see both halves of its score, the raw counts, its error rate, its record and a link to every decision it made.

Rules of backgammon, as the prompt states them

The rules as every player reads them in the prompt (bg-v2, the current version: Method: The prompt), quoted unchanged.

Setup

The board has 24 points in a horseshoe-shaped track, 12 on each side. Each player numbers the points from their own side, 1 to 24, and moves from the 24-point toward the 1-point; the two players move in opposite directions. Each player starts with 15 checkers: two on the 24-point, five on the 13-point (the midpoint), three on the 8-point, and five on the 6-point.

Points 1-6 are your home board and points 7-12 your outer board. The 7-point is the bar point.

Moving

  • Starting: each player rolls one die. The higher number moves first, playing both dice. Equal numbers are rolled again. After that, players alternate, rolling two dice each turn.
  • Using the dice: move one checker by each die. One checker may take both moves, as long as each step lands on a legal point. A roll of 6-3 moves one checker six and another (or the same) checker three.
  • Doubles are played four times: 5-5 is four moves of five.
  • Using the whole roll: you must use both dice if you can. If only one die can be played, you must play the higher one when possible. If neither can be played, the turn is lost.

Hitting and entering

  • A checker may land on an empty point, a point holding your own checkers, or a point holding exactly one opposing checker (a blot). Landing on a blot hits it and sends it to the bar.
  • A checker may never land on a point held by two or more opposing checkers, so no point ever holds checkers of both players.
  • A player with checkers on the bar must enter them all before moving anything else. Checkers enter in the opponent's home board: a 1 enters on your 24-point (the opponent's 1-point), a 2 on your 23-point, and so on up to a 6 on your 19-point. Entering on a blot hits it.
  • If no die matches an open point, you dance and the turn is lost. Against a closed board you cannot enter at all until the opponent opens a point.

Bearing off

Once all 15 of your checkers are in your home board, you may bear them off: a 1 removes a checker from the 1-point, a 2 from the 2-point, and so on. A die higher than your highest occupied point removes a checker from that highest point. For example, with 6-5, no checkers on the 6-point, and two on the 5-point, both checkers come off the 5-point. A checker hit during the bear-off must enter and come home before bearing off continues.

Scoring

The first player to bear off all 15 checkers wins:

GammonThe loser has borne off no checkers2
BackgammonThe loser has borne off no checkers and still has one on the bar or in the winner's home board3

How to run it

  1. Snapshot the league database: python3 tools/site/snapshot.py v3.db STATE (one read transaction; the prompts each model was sent go to STATE/prompts.db; the Nackgammon knockout's matches, from knockout-nack.db next to it, to STATE/knockout.db).
  2. Score every legal play of every decision with GNUbg 1.08 at 2-ply: tools/site/score.sh STATE/snap.db STATE/plays.
  3. Build and check the site: python3 tools/site/build.py --state STATE --out OUT. The build fails, and nothing is deployed, if a number does not trace to a data file, counts do not add up, leagues or versions mix, a published finding no longer holds, or a file contains a private address or credential.

The scripts are in the jevgammon repository under tools/site/; the fixed thresholds are in thresholds.json.

Late entries

A late entry joins an original league table after it was played and plays every setup of that table once, in matches of its own. Its games count in no standings table, no league table, no Hype Cup input and no other setup's numbers. The numbers here come from its own late-entry games only, computed as for a setup in a league. Late entries are ranked on the risk leaderboard; they do not change league match-points standings, Cups, or other setups' results.

Error rate and risk judgement: 95% intervals, whole games resampled. Ability d′ and caution c at the 0.001 margin: rates corrected by adding 0.5 to the counts and 1 to the totals; 95% intervals, blocks of two seat-swapped games resampled. Cut off: answers that stopped at an output limit the request sent (finish reason length, stop reason max_tokens, finishReason MAX_TOKENS, incomplete max_output_tokens), in the stored response or error. Effort: the reasoning effort the requests sent. Engine's best play: the share of its scored decisions that chose the engine's best play (equity loss under the best-play threshold). Time: median request seconds per decision (waits between retries not included). Cost per 1,000 decisions: the recorded tokens at the price shown, as labelled there (list or billed price; for a setup on our GPUs the hosted model's list price, not billed).
