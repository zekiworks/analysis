Verdict and learning lift

No arm reached the pre-registered signal threshold (plan section 8).

Signal rule (plan section 8, pre-registered), per arm other than no memory, averaged over its series: risks found after game 10 minus before game 1 at least +10 points, and right safety after game 10 minus before game 1 at least −5 points.

  • full records: threshold not reached. Risks found +3.3 points (95% interval 2.6 to 4.1); right safety +1.3 points (95% interval 0.8 to 1.8).
  • own lessons: threshold not reached. Risks found +8.3 points (95% interval 7.1 to 9.4); right safety +1.1 points (95% interval 0.6 to 1.6).
  • budget (empty: no game fit): threshold not reached. Risks found +2.8 points (95% interval 1.7 to 3.7); right safety +0.8 points (95% interval 0.4 to 1.0).

Before game 1 (T0) is one run: the after-game-10 minus before-game-1 intervals are conditional on that single observed T0. This does not apply to the game-lift intervals.

budget (empty: no game fit): its context was empty at all 100 of 100 points (no whole game record fit within the own lessons arm's token count). The plan's fixed reading (section 11): with this budget, record memory could not be provided, not "record memory didn't help".

All observed game-lift differences are below the no memory arm. For full records and own lessons, the intervals of the difference include zero. The budget (empty: no game fit) arm's interval does not include zero, although budget (empty: no game fit) and no memory had the same memory policy (an empty context) and the same dice seeds; that does not make their realized game prompts the same, as the games diverge once a play differs. The source of this unexpected difference and the calibration of the intervals were not determined in this pilot.

How the evaluation requests were made

Sent with every evaluation request:

  • temperature 1.0;
  • max_tokens 1;
  • logprobs, with top_logprobs 20;
  • the assistant turn prefilled with "Answer:", with continue_final_message;
  • enable_thinking false;
  • no top_p, top_k or seed sent.

The server's effective defaults (a read-only check of the serving machine): vLLM's log says its default sampling parameters are overridden by the model's generation_config.json, temperature 1.0, top_k 64, top_p 0.95; that file is identical on the server and in our copy of the model. No seed was sent; the server's seed behaviour was not checked.

The play is the argmax of the returned option-letter probabilities (several tokens per letter are summed). The cause of differences between empty-context runs was not verified.

Checked in the stored records of T0, the 5 retests and the 20 budget evaluations: all 5,200 answers have probability_source "logprobs"; all 7,202 requests are the prefill readout (max_tokens 1, continue_final_message), and none carries response_format, so the constrained-letter fallback never fired; no request errors. Whether vLLM returned raw or processed logprobs (before or after temperature/top_k/top_p) cannot be shown from the stored records; it was not determined.

Positions with more than 26 options (32 of the 200) are asked in groups, then among the group winners. The retests ran with 4 requests in flight, like T0; the budget (empty: no game fit) evaluations with 1.

Lessons the model wrote

The own lessons arm only. Each series starts with no lessons; after each game the model wrote its lessons, which the next game read.

Series 1: lessons after games 1-10

  • Series 1, game 1: won 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Be more aggressive when hitting; don't miss opportunities to put the opponent on the bar to gain tempo.
- Prioritize clearing mid-point checkers and bringing them home rather than burying checkers deep in the home board.
- Avoid making unnecessary hits in the endgame if they don't improve your position or hinder the opponent's entry.
- When bearing off, focus on maximizing the number of checkers removed rather than creating unnecessary points.
- Balance safety and risk; don't default to the safest move when a slightly more aggressive play significantly increases equity.

  • Series 1, game 2: won 2 points (gammon). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize hitting the opponent's blots in the early game, even if it means leaving a vulnerability.
- Avoid "burying" checkers deep in the home board too early; maintain flexibility and utilize mid-point checkers.
- When the opponent has a checker on the bar, prioritize cleaning up your own blots over making redundant internal points.
- Be more aggressive with "risk" plays when the engine suggests a hitting sequence over a passive safety move.
- Maintain a balance between closing out the opponent and bringing your remaining checkers home efficiently.

  • Series 1, game 3: won 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Stop playing too safely; prioritize hitting the opponent's blots to disrupt their timing and gain tempo.
- Look for opportunities to advance your back checkers early rather than focusing solely on building a home board.
- Avoid "burying" checkers deep in your home board when you still have active threats or runners to manage.
- When the opponent has a checker on the bar or is vulnerable, be more aggressive in attacking their anchors.
- Prioritize clearing your outermost points and bringing checkers home over making redundant points deep in the board.

  • Series 1, game 4: won 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize escaping back checkers over making internal points in the early game.
- Don't be afraid to take calculated risks to hit the opponent and disrupt their structure.
- Avoid "burying" checkers on the 2-point or 3-point when you still have checkers to bring home.
- Maintain flexibility with your mid-point and outer board checkers rather than rushing to lock down the home board.
- Be aggressive in challenging the opponent's anchor; missed hitting opportunities often lead to significant equity loss.

  • Series 1, game 5: won 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize escaping your back checkers over making redundant internal points in the early game.
- Don't be afraid to take risks by hitting blots in the outfield when it creates tempo or disrupts the opponent's structure.
- Avoid burying checkers deep in your home board while you still have assets to move from the midpoint.
- Be alert for "hit and run" opportunities that can shift the momentum of the game.
- When bearing off against a defender on the bar, maintain a flexible structure to avoid leaving shots.

  • Series 1, game 6: won 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize cleaning up blots and strengthening the home board over making redundant points.
- When playing doubles, look for the most flexible distribution of checkers rather than just stacking points.
- Be cautious not to leave unnecessary blots when the opponent has a strong anchor or checkers poised to hit.
- In the bear-off, prioritize clearing the highest points first to maximize flexibility for future rolls.
- Avoid "over-playing" safety when a slightly more aggressive move significantly increases winning equity.

  • Series 1, game 7: lost 2 points (gammon). Lessons written after this game:

    - Avoid hitting haphazardly when your home board is weak; prioritize advancing back checkers to establish a secure escape route.
- When entering from the bar, evaluate whether hitting in the outfield is more valuable than securing a landing spot or establishing an anchor.
- In the late game, focus on clearing your furthest checkers first to avoid getting trapped or leaving unnecessary blots.
- Don't prioritize making points in the home board when you still have checkers trapped in the opponent's home board.
- Maintain a balance between aggressive hitting and safety; avoid "over-hitting" if it leaves your own structure vulnerable.

  • Series 1, game 8: lost 2 points (gammon). Lessons written after this game:

    - Don't neglect your back checkers; escaping them early is often more valuable than reinforcing an internal board.
- Avoid "burying" checkers on the 1-point too early if you still have assets to mobilize or threats to neutralize.
- Be cautious with aggressive hits (wrong risks) when you are already ahead in the race or have a secure position.
- Prioritize flexibility over safety; avoid making redundant moves that don't improve your overall board structure.
- When forced to move checkers into your home board, ensure they are distributed to maximize bearing-off efficiency later.

  • Series 1, game 9: lost 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize escaping your back checkers early; don't focus on making inner-board points while your runners are trapped.
- Avoid "burying" checkers deep in the home board too early; keep them flexible on higher points to maintain control and timing.
- When the opponent is anchored in your home board, prioritize clearing your remaining outer-board points rather than making redundant internal points.
- Be careful with aggressive hits in the endgame if they leave you vulnerable or don't significantly improve your winning chances.
- In the closing stages, maximize the efficiency of your checkers to ensure you don't leave gaps or awkward distributions during the bear-off.

  • Series 1, game 10: lost 2 points (gammon). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize escaping your back checkers over making passive inner-board points in the early game.
- Avoid "burying" checkers on the 2-point early; keep your flexibility to challenge the opponent's movement.
- When you have the opportunity to advance your runners, take the risk to move them toward safety rather than playing overly conservatively.
- Don't be afraid to leave temporary blots to improve your overall board structure and racing position.
- Recognize that safety in the home board is often less valuable than mobility and timing in the outfield.
Series 2: lessons after games 1-10

  • Series 2, game 1: lost 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize hitting the opponent to gain tempo and disrupt their structure, especially when you have the opportunity to put them on the bar.
- Avoid over-stacking a single point (e.g., the 4-point) when you could be developing other assets or controlling the outer board.
- Maintain flexibility with mid-point checkers rather than rushing them home prematurely.
- Be more aggressive in the mid-game; missing multiple "hit" opportunities often leads to a significant loss in equity.
- During the bear-off, prioritize clearing the highest points first and avoid leaving gaps that could hinder your efficiency.

  • Series 2, game 2: lost 2 points (gammon). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize splitting your back checkers early to gain mobility and flexibility.
- Look for aggressive hitting opportunities to disrupt the opponent's structure, even when it feels risky.
- Avoid making unnecessary hits in your home board if they don't improve your position or secure a win.
- When leading or in the endgame, focus on safely bringing checkers home rather than creating redundant points.
- Do not ignore the value of clearing high points and advancing outfield checkers to avoid getting trapped.

  • Series 2, game 3: won 2 points (gammon). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize escaping the back checkers; don't leave them trapped while trying to attack or shore up the home board.
- Avoid taking unnecessary risks (like hitting deep in the home board) when you already have a significant positional advantage.
- When playing a holding game or escaping, look for opportunities to hit and disrupt the opponent's timing rather than playing too safely.
- Use doubles to maximize flexibility and mobility across the whole board, not just for making a single point.
- In the bearing-off phase, prioritize clearing the highest points first to avoid getting checkers stuck or leaving blots.

  • Series 2, game 4: lost 1 point (single). Lessons written after this game:

    - Prioritize escaping your back checkers over making deep internal points early in the game.

The definitions are the site's (tools/site/analysis.py). Every share is taken over valid answers.

For each arm and evaluation (T0 shared; T5 and T10 for records, lessons and budget):

  • Error rate (PR): 500 × the mean 2-ply equity loss.
  • Risk judgement: 50 × (right risks ÷ Risk pays + right safety ÷ Safety pays).
  • Risks found: right risks ÷ Risk pays positions.
  • Right safety: right safety calls ÷ Safety pays positions.
  • Risk appetite: the share of the Risk pays and Safety pays positions where the chosen play leaves more hitting rolls than the best safe play, right or wrong.
  • Invalid answers: answers that name no option. They are left out of every quality number, as on the site. Failed requests are counted separately.
  • Tokens per decision.
  • Records dropped: earlier games of the series whose full record was left out of the context. This applies to records (over the context limit) and to budget (over the lessons arm's token count).
  • Cost ($0 API spend; local compute cost not estimated) and time.

For each arm, from its games:

  • the error rate of every game;
  • decision latency;
  • tokens per decision;
  • wins.

A17 learning lift: for each arm and series, the error rate in games 1–3 minus the error rate in games 8–10. Decisions are pooled within each group of games. A positive value means the arm played better later. It is compared with the control arm's change, paired by series: (arm's lift − control's lift), averaged over the series.

Secondary: records against lessons against budget.

All of these are descriptive and outside the Holm correction. Each interval is a 95% percentile bootstrap: 10,000 resamples of whole series, fixed seed, with the same series drawn for every arm, so each resample keeps the dice schedules paired across arms.

8. Signal rule

A17 fixed this rule before the first model call. It replaces every earlier version.

For each non-control arm (records, lessons, budget), averaged over its series:

risks found after game 10 (T10) − risks found before game 1 (T0) ≥ +10 points, AND right safety (T10) − right safety (T0) ≥ −5 points.

  • Points are percentage points.
  • T0 is the shared run.
  • The rule is reported for every arm, with the learning lift against control next to it as the second measure.

9. Leakage, isolation and publication

Leakage and isolation:

  • Evaluation positions never appear in any context, record or lesson input. A game decision on a board equal to an evaluation position's board (mover's side, any dice) is left out, and the number left out is counted.
  • Evaluation answers and their scores never feed any context, lesson call or later prompt.
  • The arms never see each other's games. Each arm's context comes only from its own game records in the current series. The budget arm reads only the lessons arm's token count, never its content.
  • Pilot data are kept apart from the leagues and the site's inputs. They are not in v3.db, not under tools/site/content, and not in any public export.

Display:

  • The page shows the protocol, the lesson prompt with its hash, this signal rule and the commitment sentence before the first model call.
  • Numbers (a table per arm and evaluation, and progress as series done out of planned) update after each series.
  • No verdict ("learns" / "doesn't learn") and no learning lift are published until all planned series of every arm are done, even if games 8–10 already exist for some series.
  • Every lesson the model wrote is published next to the game it came from.
  • Learning tab order (amended before any model call, 7 Nov): one model, Gemma 4 31B, is tested across 4 arms, so the tab orders arms, not models, and only after all planned series are done. Before that it shows no ranking or leader: only "running, n of N series" and per-arm interim numbers.
    • Order: the non-control arms that meet the signal rule (section 8) come first, then those that do not.
    • Within each group, arms are ordered by the learning lift minus control's lift, higher first.
    • The control arm is the reference and is not ranked.
