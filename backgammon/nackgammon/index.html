Nackgammon has the rules of backgammon; only the starting position differs. Each setup was asked 200 Nackgammon positions and 200 standard positions matched to them by phase, position class, number of legal plays and the gap between the two best plays. Error rate (PR) = 500 × mean GNUbg 2-ply equity loss. Drop = Nackgammon PR − standard PR on the same pairs; positive = worse on Nackgammon. A setup whose first-look 97.5% interval was wide and contained 0 was asked the 300 reserve pairs too (500 pairs). Every recorded position is scored: an invalid, timed-out or missing answer scores the position's Random error, with the sensitivity checks below. Label from the final 97.5% interval: kept (within ±5 PR), dropped (the data's “worse”), better or unclear. “Unclear” means the interval is too wide to decide; it is not evidence of equivalence.

A larger drop is consistent with relying on familiar positions but is not proof: Nackgammon positions also differ in structure. “Kept” is a valid result.

The paired test's standard-side prompt differs from the league prompt (no opening table; the starting setup on its own line), so the standard error rates here are not compared with league error rates.

Random's own drop: −4.56 PR on the main 200 pairs, −3.73 on all 500 (Random scores slightly better on the Nackgammon positions). It is shown next to every result. Random is computed exactly from GNUbg's 2-ply values (mean loss over all legal plays): it is never asked and is not extended by the stopping rule; it is reported on the main 200 pairs and on all 500.

No group (exploratory), in no group comparison: GPT-6.1 Terra (a setup that had played in no league before this test, §10.14(b)); Gemini 4 Argon (low), added by Can's decision of 2026-10-28 instead of Gemini 4 Argon (high), not because of the Cup roster (§10.14(h)).
