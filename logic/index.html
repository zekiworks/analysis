Logic & Math

Can a decision model reason?

Our logic and math test: 1,125 logic questions (numerical reasoning (524), verbal reasoning (469), formal logic (132)) and 2,163 math questions in 13 topics, answered by 32 decision models, with chat models for comparison. A decision model scores each answer option and picks one; a chat model writes a reply that ends in a letter. Every question is five-option multiple choice, text only, scored against the answer key of one of six published Turkish question books (not named here), and asked in English translation. About a third of the logic questions and nearly all math questions are past ÖSYM (Turkish national exam) questions, which are public, so models may have seen them. The measures and the method are those of the team's decision-models benchmark (Method).

138 runs with answers so far, 132 of them decision-model runs. The bar above opens one test (Logic, Math, or Logic and Math, both pooled), one model group and one measure at a time.

How the scores work

  • One question per request. A decision model scores the five options and picks one; a chat model writes a reply that ends in a letter.
  • Accuracy is right / all questions. No partial credit; a reply without a letter counts as wrong.
  • Uncertainty is shown. Intervals are 95% Wilson intervals; two setups are tied unless a paired exact test (McNemar) separates them after Holm's correction.
  • Costs are what a run would cost. The provider's price × the usage its API reported (unmarked), the maker's list price × our token counts (“list”), or for our own GPU its wall-clock time from first to last answer while it was running (pauses left out), × $2.49/h (“at most”: runs that shared the GPU are each charged in full).
  • The questions are English translations from six published Turkish question books; about a third of the logic questions and nearly all math questions are past public questions of ÖSYM, Turkey's national exam centre.
