Method · Data

What the test is

Our logic and math test: 1,125 logic questions (numerical reasoning (524), verbal reasoning (469), formal logic (132)) and 2,163 math questions in 13 topics, answered by 52 decision models, with chat models for comparison. A decision model scores each answer option and picks one; a chat model writes a reply that ends in a letter. Every question is five-option multiple choice, text only, scored against the answer key of one of six published Turkish question books (not named here), and asked in English translation. About a third of the logic questions and nearly all math questions are past ÖSYM (Turkish national exam) questions, which are public, so models may have seen them. The measures and the method are those of the team's decision-models benchmark (Method).

382 runs with answers so far, 216 of them decision-model runs.

How it was built

The questions were parsed from the question books; set problems and every question that needs a figure are left out. A model translated each one into English, with automatic checks (option keys, digits, LaTeX, tables, labels, leftover untranslated words); a text that still failed is flagged and not asked. Every setup answers every question once, through the same runner as the decision-models benchmark. Sources · Translation · Runs · Repeat checks (Experiments: a second run of a setup, against its first).

How the scores work

Accuracy is the share of questions answered right, with its 95% Wilson interval; non-hallucination is the share of the answers that were not right that were an honest “I don't know” (a sixth option F, in a separate run); confidence counts the answers a decision model was at least 99% sure of and how many of them were wrong; cost and latency are per question. Five points on the front page · every measure in Method.

  • Decision Score (AA formula): Artificial Analysis's formula applied to our questions. Not their Index. 50 × (1 + accuracy − chosen-answer Brier), the chosen-answer Brier over the probability of the chosen answer, on decision models' standard runs only. Chat models and the “I don't know” runs are excluded.

How to read the test pages

What the charts and tables of each test page show:

  • Logic, Math: Five options each (A–E). Decision-model setups score every option and pick one; chat-model setups write a reply with an answer letter.
  • Logic and Math: This test is not a third question set: it pools the 1,125 Logic questions and the 2,163 Math questions into one set of 3,288 questions and scores every model on all of them together. Only models that have finished both Logic and Math are included; the others are listed below as not in Logic and Math yet. Five options each (A–E). Decision-model setups score every option and pick one; chat-model setups write a reply with an answer letter.
  • Every setup, every measure (the full table): One row per setup in accuracy order. Tie group: setups that share a letter are not told apart by the exact McNemar test on the questions both answered, Holm-corrected over every pair in this group (tb-next's compact letter display). “Not measured”: chat runs return no option probabilities and have no “I don't know” run yet; a run without a recorded cost shows —.
  • Non-hallucination, (a) Probabilistic confidence: Five options, the standard run: how many answers a setup's own probability lets it keep at ≥99% and how many of those were wrong, and how well the probability separates right from wrong answers (AUROC).
  • Non-hallucination, (b) Explicit “I don't know” option: A sixth option F on the same questions (mode dont_know runs): non-hallucination = F / (F + wrong), with the attempt rate (right + wrong) / N and the accuracy when answering right / (right + wrong). A setup that works with probabilities may rarely choose F; read it on (a) and on the comparison with its own confidence.
  • I don't know: A sixth option F on the same questions (mode dont_know runs): non-hallucination = F / (F + wrong), the attempt rate (right + wrong) / N, the accuracy when answering right / (right + wrong), the counts, and the comparison with the same setup's five-option run held back by its own confidence.

Data

Every aggregate number is in results.json (per test, Logic, Math and Logic and Math, and per setup: accuracy with its Wilson interval, right and N, Decision Score and chosen-answer Brier, AUROC, non-hallucination, cost with its basis and median latency; no question text, question ids or replies) and on these pages: the leaderboard, the full table of each test page (Every setup, every measure), and each run's record (prompt, parameters, cost basis) on Method.
